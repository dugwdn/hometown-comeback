# Hometown Comeback
## Game Design Document: Main Street Shops & Characters

---

## Player Character Backstory

You grew up in Millbrook, but life took you elsewhere. Whether it was career, family, or just the pull of somewhere new, you've spent the better part of two decades building a life away from home. Now, at a point where you're ready for something different—maybe quieter, maybe more meaningful—you've decided to come back. The old Main Street is barely recognizable, weathered and quiet, but something about its faded charm keeps drawing your eye. You have an idea, some savings, and the feeling that maybe this town needs you as much as you need it.

---

## The Five Shops: Unlock Order & Full Details

### 1. Ruby's Diner
**Why First:** It's the heart of the town. Warm, welcoming, and the perfect entry point for players to feel at home rebuilding Millbrook.

#### Shop History
**Heyday (1975-1995):** Ruby's was the place everyone met for breakfast before school, lingered over coffee at lunch, and grabbed pie after the movie. Red vinyl booths, a gleaming chrome counter, and a jukebox that never stopped playing made it feel like the town's living room.

**Current State:** The sign hangs crooked. The windows are cloudy. Inside, the booths are cracked, the counter is dull, and the jukebox has been silent for five years. But the bones are good, and the smell of decades of frying is baked into the walls—not in a bad way.

#### Townsperson: Ruby Clearwater
- **Age:** 68
- **Personality:** Ruby is the keeper of kindness. She remembers everyone's name, their usual order, and what's bothering them. She laughs easily and has a talent for making anyone feel like family. Slightly forgetful about the business side of things, but her heart is pure gold.
- **Memorable Catchphrase:** *"Sit down, eat up, and tell me what's new—the best medicine is pie and gossip, in that order."*
- **Role in Game:** Ruby wants to reopen the diner but can't do it alone. She's been running the counter at the hardware store just to make ends meet. Players will partner with her to bring the diner back, and she becomes a key source of town history and gossip.

#### Story Arc

**Part 1 - "Rust & Remembering"**
Ruby confesses that she's been afraid the diner is too far gone. Players help her clean out the storage room and discover old photographs, menus, and newspaper clippings from the diner's glory days. Ruby shares stories—heartfelt and funny—about the people who made the diner special.

**Part 2 - "The Fixing"**
The real work begins. Players help restore the counter, fix the booths, and get the kitchen equipment working. Ruby reveals she's been saving a secret: the original jukebox might still be fixable. Working together, they track down the old owner who still lives nearby, and he comes back to help restore it.

**Part 3 - "The Grand Reopening"**
Ruby's Diner reopens with a soft opening for friends. The jukebox plays. Old customers return. Ruby's gets its first new customers in years—including some of the younger folks she never thought would come back. The final scene is Ruby standing behind the counter, watching the booths fill up, with quiet joy on her face.

#### Nostalgia Detail
**The Jukebox:** A vintage 1970s Wurlitzer jukebox that still has the original 45s loaded inside. Early on, players discover it hidden in the back room, and there's a mini-quest to find the one person in town who knows how to restore it.

#### Decorating Style Options
1. **Classic Retro (1970s Diner):** Harvest gold and burnt orange accents, wood paneling, patterned wallpaper with swags and shades, chrome fixtures
2. **Modernized Nostalgia (1980s-90s refresh):** Lighter wood, pastel pinks and teals, cleaner lines, but keeping the old counter and booths as centerpieces
3. **Cozy Contemporary:** Cream and soft colors, vintage reproductions mixed with modern comfort, keeping the character but making it feel livable

#### Seasonal Event
**Autumn Pie Festival** (September-October)
Ruby hosts a pie-making competition and tasting event. Players can help prepare, decorate, or even enter their own recipe. Townspeople gather, stories are shared, and it becomes an annual tradition that brings the community together. Great place for relationship-building between characters.

### 2. The Melody Record Shop
**Why Second:** Music is universal comfort. This shop builds on the community energy from the diner and brings a new dimension—nostalgia, discovery, and deep conversation.

#### Shop History
**Heyday (1978-2000):** The Melody was where music lovers came to hunt for records, swap recommendations, and discover new artists. The owner curated an eclectic collection, and the listening station in the back was where friendships formed and minds expanded. Live music on Friday nights drew crowds.

**Current State:** The windows are papered over. Inside, the wooden bins are warped. Records remain, but they're dusty, organized haphazardly. The listening station is gone. There's a hole in the roof that the owner has been catching rain with buckets for two years. The place smells like mildew and heartbreak.

#### Townsperson: Marcus Webb
- **Age:** 52
- **Personality:** Marcus is thoughtful and a bit dreamy. He speaks slowly, as if every word matters. He has strong opinions about music but delivers them kindly. There's a sadness to him—a life that didn't quite go the way he planned—but he finds comfort in vinyl and melody.
- **Memorable Catchphrase:** *"It's all in the music, you know. Listen close enough, and it'll tell you everything you need to know."*
- **Role in Game:** Marcus is the shop's owner who couldn't keep it afloat when streaming killed record sales. He's been working as a maintenance man at the school, but his heart never left the shop. He becomes a mentor figure to the player, teaching them about curation and about finding meaning in small things.

#### Story Arc

**Part 1 - "Dust Covers Everything"**
Players help Marcus assess the damage and begin the restoration. They discover love letters tucked into record sleeves and photographs of concert crowds from the 70s and 80s. Marcus opens up about a former partner—a musician who used to perform at the shop on Friday nights. They're no longer in contact, but their memory lingers in the store.

**Part 2 - "Needle to Groove"**
Players help Marcus catalog and organize the collection. Together, they restore the listening station and discover Marcus has been writing album reviews in a hidden notebook for years—thoughtful, detailed, beautiful pieces nobody has ever read. Players encourage him to start sharing these online, and they become a small cult classic.

**Part 3 - "The Music Returns"**
The shop reopens with a listening party and a surprise: the musician Marcus loved all those years ago finds out the shop is reopening (through the online reviews) and reaches out. They reconnect. The shop hosts its first live music event in 20 years, and it's a tender, bittersweet evening that hints at a possible second chapter for Marcus.

#### Nostalgia Detail
**Vinyl Records:** The entire collection remains, including rare pressings and cult classics from the 1970s-1990s. Players discover a pristine copy of a local band's only album from 1982, which becomes a running thread through the game.

#### Decorating Style Options
1. **Vinyl Vintage (1970s-80s Record Store):** Deep wood tones, cork bulletin board, Edison bulbs, album art as wall decor, cozy listening nook with bean bags
2. **Modern Crate Digger:** Minimalist shelving with brass accents, curated album displays, warm lighting, retro-modern aesthetic
3. **Eclectic Music Lover:** Colorful, artistic, with original posters, string lights, mismatched vintage furniture, community bulletin board, feels like a music lover's home

#### Seasonal Event
**Vinyl Revival Swap Meet** (May)
Players host a record swap and sale where townspeople and visitors bring records to buy, sell, or trade. Marcus gives mini-talks about rare finds. Players can help organize, curate displays, or host listening stations. It becomes a draw for collectors and music nerds from surrounding areas.

### 3. Rosewood Theater
**Why Third:** Theaters are gathering places and dream-weavers. This shop brings glamour and storytelling to Main Street and creates natural connections with the community so far.

#### Shop History
**Heyday (1925-2005):** The Rosewood opened in the roaring twenties and became the social center of Millbrook. Movie nights were events. The marquee glowed. Couples held hands in the balcony. The lobby had a beautiful brass ticket counter and a candy counter that sold homemade fudge.

**Current State:** The marquee lights are broken, the glass is cracked. The seats inside are ripped, and the screen is deteriorating. Water damage is visible on the ceiling. But the bones are spectacular—soaring ceilings, original art deco details, and a projection room that's still surprisingly intact. It's a beautiful skeleton of what it was.

#### Townsperson: Helen Ashford
- **Age:** 74
- **Personality:** Helen carries herself with theatrical grace, even in everyday moments. She has perfect posture, precise diction, and a way of making mundane stories feel like grand narratives. She's lonely, though she'd never admit it. Her life peaked in high school when she was the star of school plays, and she's been chasing that feeling ever since.
- **Memorable Catchphrase:** *"The show must go on, darling. Even when the curtain is dusty and the lights are dim, we must play our parts with dignity."*
- **Role in Game:** Helen is the former owner and part-time ticket seller when the theater was barely hanging on. She knows everything about the building and becomes the player's guide to understanding what the theater was. She also harbors a secret—she always wanted to direct a real production but never got the chance.

#### Story Arc

**Part 1 - "Behind the Velvet"**
Players explore the theater with Helen, who tells stories about opening nights, movie premieres, and the couples who met in the balcony (including herself and a love she lost long ago). They discover the original projection equipment still works, and Helen shows them how to thread film.

**Part 2 - "Restoration & Rehearsal"**
Players help fix the seats, clean the screen, and restore the lobby. Helen begins talking about a dream she had decades ago: a community production of a small play. She mentions it casually, but players sense this is important. Helen gains courage and starts outlining a one-act play she could direct with help from the community.

**Part 3 - "Opening Night"**
The theater reopens with Helen's production of a charming, heartfelt one-act play featuring townspeople (including some of the other characters). It's imperfectly beautiful. Helen stands in the wings with tears in her eyes. The community sees itself reflected on that stage and remembers why gathering in shared spaces matters. It's Helen's curtain call, and she's fully alive for maybe the first time in decades.

#### Nostalgia Detail
**Original Projection Equipment:** A manually-operated 35mm film projector from the 1940s that still works. Learning to operate it becomes a special skill players can unlock.

#### Decorating Style Options
1. **Art Deco Dreams (Restoration):** Brass fixtures, jewel tones (deep emerald, navy, gold), geometric patterns, period-accurate chandeliers
2. **Cozy Cinema (Modern Comfort):** Keeps the deco elements but updates with modern theater seating, contemporary lighting, maintains the grandeur but adds comfort
3. **Community Stage (Eclectic):** Vintage theater mixed with community art, gallery walls, local artist displays, makes it feel like a gathering place rather than a relic

#### Seasonal Event
**Summer Movie Nights** (July-August)
The theater becomes an outdoor screening venue on the lawn behind the building during summer. Players help set up, organize seating, prepare concessions, and run classic films from the 70s-90s. It becomes the town's favorite summer tradition, with families bringing blankets and picnics.

### 4. The Leaf & Petal Garden Shop
**Why Fourth:** Gardens represent growth and renewal—thematic for a restoration game. This shop brings natural beauty and becomes a nexus for character relationships.

#### Shop History
**Heyday (1982-2005):** The Leaf & Petal started as a small plant shop and grew into a community gardening hub. Plants filled every window and corner. The owner hosted gardening classes, plant swap meets, and grew rare specimens that drew enthusiasts from surrounding towns. It was equal parts store and sanctuary.

**Current State:** The greenhouse is collapsed on one side. Inside, it's heartbreaking—rows of dead plants, empty pots, soil spilled everywhere. But there are survivors: stubborn perennials pushing through cracks, a few plants on shelves that have somehow endured neglect, and seeds left in packets. There's potential in the wreckage.

#### Townsperson: Emma Sutton
- **Age:** 46
- **Personality:** Emma is quiet and observant. She listens more than she speaks. She has a gentle way about her, and plants seem to respond to her presence. She's been through difficult times and has learned that growth—whether in plants or people—takes time and patience. She's content with simplicity.
- **Memorable Catchphrase:** *"Everything blooms in its own time. Some flowers take longer than others, but that doesn't make them any less beautiful."*
- **Role in Game:** Emma is a part-time gardener who has been quietly tending the few plants that survived. She's been afraid to dream about the shop reopening because dreaming about good things only brings disappointment (in her experience). Players convince her that restoring the garden shop is worth attempting.

#### Story Arc

**Part 1 - "Seeds of Hope"**
Players help Emma assess what can be saved and begin clearing. They discover seeds from decades ago, including some rare heirloom varieties. Emma tells players about a community garden her mother tended, which she lost when her mother passed away. The garden shop becomes about honoring that memory.

**Part 2 - "Growing Season"**
Players help Emma establish new growing areas and propagate plants from survivors. Emma opens up about her life—not seeking pity, but sharing. Players meet her twice a week for "gardening conversations," and through these, Emma begins to believe that good things might actually happen to her. She starts designing the garden shop around the idea of community and healing.

**Part 3 - "Full Bloom"**
The garden shop reopens with a celebration during spring. Emma has established a small community garden space in the adjacent lot where townspeople can lease plots. There's a teaching greenhouse, a seed library, and a plant swap station. Emma stands in the middle of it all—surrounded by green, by growth, by people she's helped teach to garden. Her quiet joy is profound.

#### Nostalgia Detail
**Heirloom Seeds:** The shop discovers a locked cabinet of heirloom seed packets from the 1970s-80s, many from plants that people no longer grow. Players can help restore and catalog these seeds, creating a living connection to the town's botanical past.

#### Decorating Style Options
1. **Vintage Greenhouse (Classic):** Exposed wood, glass panes, vintage plant stands and propagation tables, old seed catalogs framed on walls, natural wood and iron
2. **Modern Garden (Contemporary):** Clean lines, raised beds, modern shelving with brass accents, living walls, sustainable materials, soft greens and earth tones
3. **Eclectic Garden Lover (Whimsical):** Mismatched vintage pots and furniture, colorful painted wood, DIY aesthetic, hanging plants everywhere, feels like a gardener's personal paradise

#### Seasonal Event
**Plant Swap & Seedling Sale** (April-May)
Players help Emma organize the spring event where townspeople trade plant cuttings, swaps seedlings, and participate in gardening workshops. Emma teaches classes on propagation and community gardening. It becomes the official kick-off to the growing season and a beloved community tradition.

### 5. Vintage Threads Consignment
**Why Fifth:** Fashion and clothing carry stories. This final shop brings sustainability, nostalgia, and creates the strongest web of character connections, making the town feel cohesive.

#### Shop History
**Heyday (1995-2008):** Vintage Threads opened during the rise of the 1990s retro trend. It was curated, fun, and felt like treasure hunting. The owner bought estate sales, old closets, and collections. Friday nights were buzzing with teens and young adults finding their style. The store felt like a friend's attic—organized chaos full of personality.

**Current State:** The windows display old mannequins in faded clothes. Inside, racks are packed with unsold inventory from years ago. The fitting rooms still have mirrors, though they're dusty. A few truly special pieces remain buried in boxes. The space smells like old fabric and closed drawers. It's been in slowmo decline for over a decade.

#### Townsperson: Dale Foster
- **Age:** 52
- **Personality:** Dale has an artist's eye and a collector's heart. They're charming, gossipy in the best way (remembers everyone and their stories), and genuinely interested in people. Dale has lived a full life with both joys and sorrows, and they wear them both with grace. They have a habit of telling stories about clothes—whose they were, where they came from, who wore them at important moments.
- **Memorable Catchphrase:** *"Every piece has a story, honey. Our job is just to find the next chapter."*
- **Role in Game:** Dale is the original owner, now working odd jobs to survive. They've been heartbroken over losing the shop but never quite closed it officially. They're overjoyed when the player offers to help restore it, seeing it as a second chance—for themselves, for the shop, and for clothing to find people who'll love them.

#### Story Arc

**Part 1 - "Sorting & Stories"**
Players help Dale sort through inventory, discover treasures buried in boxes, and listen to the stories. A burgundy leather jacket belonged to someone's prom date. A beaded dress was worn to a wedding in 1985. A band t-shirt belonged to a musician who moved to LA. Dale's memories are both fond and melancholic, and sharing them becomes therapeutic.

**Part 2 - "Curation & Connection"**
Players help Dale curate the shop, setting up displays that tell stories. Dale reveals that they once had a partner—a fashion designer who dreamed of creating a sustainable clothing line. The relationship didn't last, but the dream stayed. Dale begins designing "limited edition" collections with local artists and crafters, blending vintage pieces with new designs.

**Part 3 - "New Chapters"**
Vintage Threads reopens as a gallery-meets-consignment-shop. There's a small café corner (run in partnership with Ruby's). The partner Dale loved reconnects (through social media, after seeing what Dale has done with the shop) and proposes collaborating on a sustainable fashion line. Players witness Dale's joy as old dreams restart with new energy. The shop becomes a community gathering place where fashion tells the town's story.

#### Nostalgia Detail
**Fashion Through the Decades:** The inventory spans 1970s to early 2000s fashion—from disco to grunge to Y2K. Players can help organize by decade and style, learning fashion history as they explore the clothes.

#### Decorating Style Options
1. **Vintage Boutique (Gallery):** Exposed brick (or accent walls), vintage clothing on statement hangers, gallery lighting, curated display areas by decade, sophisticated vintage store aesthetic
2. **Modern Eclectic (Curated Modern):** Minimalist shelving, clean lines with pops of color, art installations featuring clothing as art, contemporary gallery feel
3. **Community Fashion Lounge (Café-Meets-Boutique):** Comfortable seating, warm lighting, café corner with Ruby's baked goods, feels like a friend's house, browsable and cozily cluttered in an intentional way

#### Seasonal Event
**Fashion Swap & Upcycling Workshop** (October)
Players help Dale organize a fashion swap where townspeople bring clothes to exchange. Dale teaches upcycling workshops (sewing classes, tie-dye, customization). Local makers sell handmade pieces. It becomes an eco-friendly, community-building event that celebrates fashion's sustainability and personal expression.

## Townspeople Connections & Community Web

### The Five Main Characters
1. **Ruby Clearwater** (68) - Diner Owner
2. **Marcus Webb** (52) - Record Shop Owner
3. **Helen Ashford** (74) - Theater Owner/Dreamer
4. **Emma Sutton** (46) - Gardener/Plant Shop Partner
5. **Dale Foster** (52) - Consignment Shop Owner

### Relationship Map

#### Ruby & Helen: Lifelong Friends
Ruby and Helen have been best friends since high school—60+ years of friendship. Helen was a theater person; Ruby wanted to run a diner. They supported each other's dreams, attended every performance, and went to the diner together constantly. Ruby knows Helen's deepest secret (that she always wanted to direct). When the player first meets them, there's a tender scene where Ruby quietly mentions that Helen is lonely and needs to believe in herself again. Ruby becomes Helen's primary cheerleader during the theater restoration.

#### Marcus & Emma: Sunday Morning Ritual
Marcus and Emma are neighbors who have created a ritual over the years: Sunday morning coffee and conversation on Emma's porch, where they sit surrounded by her plants. They've never dated (both sensed the other was nursing old heartbreak), but they're deeply connected. Marcus makes Emma laugh without trying. Emma makes Marcus feel seen. When Emma doubts the garden shop reopening, Marcus plays music that reminds her why growth is worth it. Marcus's album reviews mention Emma's plants.

#### Dale & Ruby: The Nostalgia Collectors
Ruby and Dale share a love of stories and nostalgia. Dale brings vintage diner furniture and accessories to the consignment shop (booths from closed restaurants, vintage signs, old menus). Ruby shops there regularly, buying pieces to restore the diner. Dale tells Ruby stories about who owned each piece. Ruby, in turn, tells Dale which townspeople come in to the diner and what they're going through. Their connection is one of shared history-keeping and community care.

#### Helen & Dale: Theater Connection
Helen buys clothes from Dale's shop for her theater production. Dale suggests outfits for characters and helps Helen conceptualize what the play's world should look like. There's a sweet scene where Dale tells Helen that wearing the right costume changes how you carry yourself—which helps Helen, who needs to believe she can direct. Dale recognizes the same hunger in Helen that Dale has—the hunger to create, to matter, to do the thing you've always dreamed of.

#### Emma & Ruby: Green Gifts
Emma makes floral arrangements for Ruby's diner—seasonal displays, centerpieces for the pie festival, flowers for special occasions. Ruby gives Emma advice (in Ruby's gentle way) about her tendency to assume bad things will happen. Ruby's steady optimism is a counterbalance to Emma's caution. Emma realizes through conversations with Ruby that she's spent decades waiting for things to get worse, when she could have been building things. Ruby buys plants from the garden shop and fills the diner with life.

#### Marcus & Dale (Past Romance Thread)
Marcus and Dale dated in the 1990s—an on-and-off relationship that never quite worked because both were focused on their shops. They parted on good terms, but there was always "what if?" between them. When Dale starts the fashion collaboration and is reconnecting with their old partner, Marcus feels a bittersweetness. But through conversations with Emma, Marcus realizes that closure with Dale is actually a beautiful thing—they were meant to support each other as friends, which is what's happening. There's a scene where Marcus and Dale laugh about the past while genuinely celebrating each other's second chapters.

#### All Five: The Friday Night Tradition
By the end of the game, the five characters establish a Friday Night Gathering: They rotate between venues (one week at the diner, one week at the record shop listening party, one week at the theater, etc.). They bring potluck items, Emma brings flowers from the garden shop, Dale sometimes brings a special vintage item related to the venue, Marcus plays music, Helen reads poetry, and Ruby feeds everyone. This gathering becomes the heart of the town's revival—proof that when people reconnect and invest in shared spaces, community isn't lost, it's reborn.

#### Extended Townspeople (Mentioned but Not Detailed)
- **The Theater Piano Player** - An elderly man who used to play during intermission, now in a care facility. Helen tracking him down and asking him to play at the reopening is a powerful subplot.
- **The Old Record Store Repairman** - Lives nearby, comes back to help restore the jukebox and the vinyl equipment. Becomes an unexpected mentor to Marcus.
- **Young Families** - Generic but crucial: they represent the future and are drawn to Main Street as it improves, showing that revival serves all ages.
- **The Original Fashion Designer Partner** - Dale's old love interest, who reconnects through social media and represents second chances and unfinished dreams.

## Tone & Overall Atmosphere

### Cozy, Warm, Gentle Humor
- No darkness or heavy themes—this is about gentle restoration and rediscovery
- Humor comes from character quirks, misunderstandings, and the comedy of small-town life
- Characters occasionally make self-deprecating jokes but never turn on each other
- Bittersweet moments are treated with tenderness, not tragedy (Helen's lost love, Marcus's closed chapter, Emma's grief—all healed through connection and purpose)
- Celebration of imperfection: things don't have to be pristine to be meaningful

### 1970s-1990s Nostalgia
- Visual design emphasizes the glory of pre-digital Main Streets
- References to technologies that matter: vinyl records, 35mm projection, practical gardening, handmade clothing
- The game doesn't mourn lost technology but celebrates what those eras created: communities, gathering places, and meaningful connections
- Music throughout is 70s-90s (era-appropriate for character backstories and retail ambiance)
- Fashion, décor, and products are authentic to the era
- No judgment about progress or decline—just a recognition that different eras had different gifts

### Accessibility & Inclusivity
- Characters are diverse in age, gender presentation, and life experience
- Relationships are authentic (Dale is presented naturally, no "issue episode" needed; Ruby's friendship with Helen is the spine of the narrative)
- The game acknowledges that life is imperfect (loss, loneliness, failure) but emphasizes that connection and purpose heal those wounds
- No gatekeeping around hobbies or interests (Marcus's music nerd enthusiasm, Emma's gardening, Dale's fashion history—all are welcomed and celebrated)

## How This Serves the Player (35+ Women)

1. **Seeing Yourself:** The player can project themselves onto any Main Street role—there's room for different personalities, experiences, and approaches to restoration.

2. **Meaningful Work:** Rather than endless task lists, restoration is about uncovering and honoring what already mattered in a community.

3. **Intergenerational Connections:** The game shows relationships that span decades and generations, normalizing the richness of older friendships and newer connections.

4. **Second Chances:** Every character gets one—a chance to dream again, create again, believe again. This is powerful for players at a life stage where second acts feel possible.

5. **Agency in Community:** The player isn't a savior figure; they're a catalyst. The townspeople do the real work and make the real choices. The player simply creates space for that to happen.

---

## Design Summary

This game design centers five distinct, deeply drawn characters who represent different facets of what makes a community alive: nourishment (Ruby), creativity (Marcus), imagination (Helen), growth (Emma), and expression (Dale). Their individual stories are about reclaiming dreams and overcoming doubt. Their collective story is about rediscovering the power of gathering places and human connection. The player's role is to listen, support, encourage, and create space for these transformations to unfold.

The tone is consistently warm and hopeful without being saccharine. The nostalgia is grounded in specific details that bring the 70s-90s era alive, not as a lost golden age but as a time when certain kinds of connection thrived and still can. The message, quietly, is that it's never too late to come home, start over, build community, and believe in yourself.
